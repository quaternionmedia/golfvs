| 012 | 2026-09-09 | Record floats are **quantized on write**: positions to 0.1 mm (4 dp); normalised scalars and unit-vector components to 6 dp. The quantized value is what the simulation consumes, not merely what is stored. Canonical form is hand-written JSON: sorted keys, no whitespace, fixed-width decimals, never scientific notation. | Closes `RECORD_SCHEMA.md` question 4, which required an answer before the first fixture was recorded. Quantizing at the boundary sidesteps the round-trip problem rather than solving it: nothing depends on a double surviving a decimal round trip, so neither hex floats nor 17-digit strings are needed and a record still fits in a chat message. `JSON.stringify` was rejected because its key order follows insertion and its float width follows engine precision, and the bytes are what gets hashed, so determinism must be a property of the encoder rather than of the caller. Asserted by round-tripping 2000 seeded values at exact equality. | Active |

| 015 | 2026-09-09 | The intro hole's defender is an **archer that acts only on balls whose flight would carry them out of bounds**, pinning them where the arrow lands, with no stroke penalty. Skeet remains the M2 defender and the 1.0 roster is unchanged. | The intro hole is the one hole that must not be failable. A beginner’s characteristic disaster is spraying the ball off the course, and an out-of-bounds penalty is the harshest rule in golf arriving first. An archer that stops exactly those shots turns the defender beat into a safety net, which is the same principle as choosing the lesson from the lie rather than from a step counter. It is also unusually legible: it never acts on a good shot, so when it does act the player learns what out of bounds means without being told. This departs from the framing of defenders as pure adversaries, deliberately and only on this hole — Pillar 6, kind comedy. | Active — the archer stands; the par-4 hole it guarded is superseded by ADR-017 |
| 016 | 2026-09-09 | The intro hole is rendered as a **holodeck blueprint**: a solid dark-grey deck, a cyan grid, glowing outlines, an amber course boundary drawn where it actually is, and the ball as the one lit solid object. No depicted golf course. The archer stands on top of the spire. Scenery reuses the existing signal palette rather than introducing one. | Two reasons, and the second is the load-bearing one. **Tone:** the sunlit manicured fairway sells an image of golf, and the irrigation behind it, that this project does not want to promote; a simulation that is openly a simulation says what the game actually is. **Readability:** the hole already taught through geometry, a coloured stub and a ghost hand, so it was a diagram wearing grass, and the grass was actively in the way — the archer of ADR-015 enforces a boundary the player had no way to see, and on a deck that boundary is simply the edge of the deck. Drawing it also removed the trees, which existed to hide a world edge that is now the thing worth showing. Cheap as well: the whole deck grid is fewer vertices than one of the trees it replaced. Scoped to the intro hole; whether the biomes adopt it, contrast with it, or are reached through it is left open. | Active |
| 017 | 2026-09-09 | **Three clubs and an auto-putter are ratified**, as `ClubProfile` resources: driver, iron, wedge, putter, differing by launch speed band, launch angle and curve. The first run becomes a **private practice range** with three pins at three distances, one per club, replacing the par-4 hole "The Handshake". Supersedes the intro-hole half of ADR-014 and ADR-015; the archer, the wordless rule and the blueprint look all carry over unchanged. | The two decisions are the same decision. Until now `BallFlight` held one set of numbers for every full shot, so every stroke in the game was the same club wearing whichever name the record happened to store -- three clubs could not be *taught* because they did not exist. A range is how anybody has ever learned what a club does: hit it at something, watch where it goes. It also puts the first screen squarely on M1's gate, *is it fun to hit balls at nothing, on a phone?*, where the par-4 answered a question from a later milestone. The Handshake taught power, shaping and the putt with one club and a lie-driven lesson machine; that was good work for a hole and the wrong first thing to show. Nothing is scored against par on a range, so the mode also drops the one part of the old hole that could make a beginner feel behind. | Active — the range stands; its club set is superseded by ADR-018 |
| 018 | 2026-09-09 | The club set is **long, short and putt**, and the **player chooses**, from a selector along the bottom of the screen that draws each club as a bar the length of its reach. The pin suggests a club when it comes up and never imposes one. Supersedes ADR-017's driver/iron/wedge/putter and its automatic putter; `intent.club` becomes `long | short | putt`. | Two simplifications, both about the same barrier. Golf's own vocabulary carries golf's own cost of entry: driver/iron/wedge asks a player to already know that a wedge is the short one, which is exactly the knowledge a first run cannot assume. Long, short and putt say the decision rather than the equipment, and there are three because three is how many decisions there are -- a fourth club would be a distinction without one. Making the putter selectable rather than automatic follows: an auto-putter is a rule the player has to learn is being applied to them, and on a range there is no green to trigger it. The selector is the game's only control and still has no words on it, because a bar as long as the club is far is legible faster than the word "wedge" is readable. Presses inside it are consumed by the Control, so a tap on it cannot start a stroke -- no rectangle checks in the gesture. | Active |
